4. Bank account access (PSD2 / Account Information Service)

When you connect a bank account, you are authorising read-only access to your account information through a licensed Account Information Service Provider (AISP), under the EU’s PSD2 framework. That access runs through your own account with open-banking.io (see below); Tecron is not itself an AISP. Key points:

  • Access is granted by your explicit consent, given directly to your bank via its secure authentication (Strong Customer Authentication).
  • Consent is time-limited (typically up to ~180 days) and you can withdraw it at any time — from within the Service (disconnect) or directly with your bank.
  • We only read balances and transactions. We cannot and do not initiate payments or move money.

How the connection works. You use your own account at open-banking.io, operated by Tatic ApS (Denmark), which provides account information under the licence of Enable Banking. You give your bank consent in open-banking.io’s app, and you upload to the Service a credentials file you export from open-banking.io. That file contains a key that decrypts your account data: we store it encrypted, use it only to read your balances and transactions, and delete it when you disconnect. Your relationship with open-banking.io (including its fees and its own privacy policy) is between you and open-banking.io; to fully withdraw access, also remove the connection in open-banking.io’s app.

5. Automated classification (AI — only if you turn it on)

AI classification is off unless you switch it on. We ask once after you sign in, and you can change your answer at any time in Settings → AI classification. Without it, the rules you set keep categorising your transactions as usual.

When it is on, transactions that none of your rules recognise are sent to Anthropic (the provider of the “Claude” AI model) to suggest a category: the description, amount, date, transaction type, merchant and counterparty name. Account and card numbers are removed before sending — also where they appear inside the description text — and we do not send your name or login details. If you use the budget questionnaire, your answers to it are sent the same way to draft a first budget.

This produces a suggestion only, which you can accept or change; it is not used to make decisions with legal or similarly significant effects about you.

5a. Co-parent claims (optional pack)

With the co-parent pack, a parent (the “claimant”) records the children’s extraordinary expenses — such as medical, school or activity costs — and asks the other parent for their share, as agreed between them or set by a court. Tecron records what the parents send and answer; it does not decide what counts as an extraordinary expense and gives no legal advice.

What is processed.

  • About the children: their first name only, and the expenses recorded for them. Receipts can contain health information (for example a prescription or a hospital invoice), which is a special category of personal data.
  • About the other parent: the name, email address and language the claimant enters; when they open a claim; and their answers (accepted, disputed, paid) and messages, each with its date and time.
  • About the claimant: the expenses, receipts, claims and messages they create, as part of their account.

Why, and on what basis. The pack exists so a parent can settle a cost the law shares between both parents (Article 203bis of the Belgian Civil Code). Receipts with health information are processed because they are needed to establish or exercise that claim (Art. 9(2)(f) GDPR). The other parent’s details are processed in the legitimate interest of both parents in settling those costs, and are used for nothing else.

What the other parent sees. They do not need an account. Each claim is sent as a personal link that opens that claim only — its expenses, receipts and history — and nothing else of the claimant’s finances. A link works for 60 days and the claimant can revoke it. The emails we send contain only names, the period, the amount, the due date and any message one parent writes to the other: never a receipt or a description of an expense. The other parent gets at most one reminder; not answering is never treated as agreement.

How it is protected. Receipts are encrypted (AES-256) before they are stored. Co-parent data is never sent to the AI provider, whether or not AI classification is on.

How long it is kept. As long as the claimant keeps it. Expenses that have not been sent can be changed or deleted at any time. A sent claim is kept unchanged, because it is a record both parents rely on; it is deleted when the claimant removes the other parent from their account or closes their account. Receipts are deleted together with their expense.

If you are the other parent. You have the rights listed in section 9 and can contact us at admin@tecron.be. We will correct or delete your contact details on request. A claim that was already sent may be kept where it is needed to establish or defend a legal claim (Art. 17(3)(e) GDPR); in that case we will tell you so.

7. How long we keep your data

We keep your account and financial data for as long as your account is active. If you close your account, we delete or anonymise your personal data within a reasonable period. Temporary data (such as 2FA codes) is auto-expired within minutes.

Invoices are the exception. Belgian law requires us to keep accounting records, including invoices and the billing details on them, for seven years. Those are retained for that period even after you close your account, and are not deleted on request — the legal basis is our legal obligation, not your consent. Nothing else about your account is kept under that exception: your transactions, budgets and classifications are deleted or anonymised with the rest.

8. How we protect your data

  • Sensitive secrets (such as 2FA secrets and bank-consent tokens) are encrypted at rest (AES-256); passwords are stored only as salted hashes.
  • Backups are encrypted before they leave the server, with a key that is kept offline and is not stored on the server itself.
  • Access is protected by authentication, two-factor authentication and rate limiting.
  • Application infrastructure is private and not exposed to the public internet beyond what is strictly necessary to operate the Service.

8a. What Tecron can see

Your data is stored on our servers in a form the Service can read, because it has to: it imports your transactions, applies your rules and calculates your budget for you. That means that, as the operator, we have technical access to the data you store. We use that access only to operate and secure the Service and to help you when you ask us to. We do not browse your data, and we never sell it.

Tecron does not use end-to-end encryption. If that matters to you, tell us at admin@tecron.be — it is something we would consider if there is demand for it.
